What is RTP?

Return to Player (RTP) is the percentage of all wagered money a slot pays back to players over millions of spins. It's calculated by game providers and certified by independent auditors. RTP is not a guarantee for short sessions – you can win or lose in 100 spins. Over thousands of spins, results trend toward the RTP. A 96.5% RTP slot keeps 3.5% as house edge. RTP vs Volatility – What's the Difference?

RTP is long-term payback. Volatility (variance) is how often and how big wins land. Low volatility: smaller wins, more frequent. High volatility: rare wins, but can be massive. Both slots can have 96.5% RTP – volatility only affects the pattern. Choose based on preference: steady action (low vol) or chase big wins (high vol). A low-volatility slot might pay small amounts every few spins; a high-volatility slot might go 50+ spins without a win, then hit a 100x multiplier. Your bankroll lasts longer on low-volatility games; high-volatility games need a bigger budget to ride out dry spells.

Can Casinos Change RTP?

Some providers offer multiple RTP configurations – e.g. 94%, 96%, 98%. The casino chooses which version to offer. Reputable casinos like PHP777 typically offer the higher RTP versions. Unlicensed sites might use lower RTP builds to increase house edge. Always play at licensed casinos where RTP is disclosed and audited. You can't change RTP yourself – it's set by the game. What you can do is choose games with better RTP and avoid those with poor payback.
